Science & judge notes

Research question

Can a composite made from locally available organic waste influence the movement of a visible tracer through sandy soil?

Potential value

Explores reuse of coffee grounds, tea waste and date-palm residues in a low-cost material concept.

Next experiment

Measure actual dissolved metal concentrations before and after treatment using an approved laboratory method.

What the experiment can and cannot show

It can show: the visible difference between the collected blue-tracer water from the two setups under the conditions tested.

It cannot show: which chemical mechanism caused the colour difference, whether any heavy metals were removed, or whether the effluent is safe.

Important limitation: without repeated trials and additional controls, differences in flow, contact time, sand packing and dye behaviour may affect the result.

Suggested answer to judges

“Our initial experiment used blue food colouring as a safe visual tracer to compare two soil columns. We observed that the control effluent was dark blue, while the tile-column effluent appeared almost clear. This is preliminary evidence of a difference in visible tracer behaviour, not proof of heavy-metal immobilisation. Our next step would be to conduct repeated tests and measure actual metal concentrations in an approved laboratory.”

Safety and disposal

Do not use this prototype to treat drinking water. If a future experiment uses actual heavy metals, work only under teacher or laboratory supervision and follow approved waste-disposal procedures. Do not compost a tile exposed to metals unless its safety has been established.
